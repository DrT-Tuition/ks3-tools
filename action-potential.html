    Resting: the pump moves 3 Na⁺ out for 2 K⁺ in; K⁺ leaks out faster than Na⁺ leaks in.

    Depolarisation: voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open; Na⁺ diffuses in.

    Repolarisation: Na⁺ channels inactivated; voltage-gated K⁺ channels open; K⁺ diffuses out.

    Hyperpolarisation: K⁺ channels are slow to close; too much K⁺ diffuses out.

    Return to resting: K⁺ channels close; the pump restores the resting potential.

    Faster impulses: myelinated (saltatory conduction, node to node); thicker axon; warmer.

    Myelin insulates the membrane, so ions cannot diffuse across it except at the nodes of Ranvier.

    Model limits: Ion numbers are tiny and symbolic; real concentration changes are small. Timings are slowed right down: a real action potential lasts about 1–2 ms. Channel shapes are schematic, and the trace is an idealised curve. The pump works all the time; the model only moves ions through it after an action potential. In the race the myelinated axon is 10× faster; real saltatory conduction can be up to about 50× faster.
