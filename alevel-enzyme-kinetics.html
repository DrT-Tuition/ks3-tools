Q₁₀ (temperature coefficient) = R₂ ÷ R₁, where R₂ is the rate at a temperature 10 °C higher than R₁’s.

Rate from a tangent = gradient = change in y ÷ change in x. Choose two points far apart on the tangent. Units: y units per x unit (cm³ s⁻¹).

Percentage change = (new value − original value) ÷ original value × 100.

Reading a value: go up from the x value to the curve, then across to the y axis. Count the small squares.

Tangent at t = 0: a straight line from the origin with the same slope as the curve at the very start. It touches the curve there and does not cut across it.

About this model

The enzyme, substrate and inhibitor shapes are schematic, not to scale, and the change of shape is exaggerated. The rate graphs use invented arbitrary units (AU) with the shapes found in experiments; real optima vary between enzymes. In the experiment the readings are invented from a smooth curve (volume = V∞(1 − e−kt)), each run varies by a few per cent, and real runs have more scatter and a short delay before the first bubbles reach the syringe. Real catalase needs much more hydrogen peroxide to approach its Vmax than the model shows.
