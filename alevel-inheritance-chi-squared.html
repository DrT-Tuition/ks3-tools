About this model

Each cross follows one or two genes with complete dominance unless it says otherwise; real characteristics are often controlled by many genes and by the environment. Breed draws each offspring at random from the probabilities of its cross, so the numbers change every time, just as in a real cross. The Punnett square assumes the gametes are equally likely, which is true for genes on different chromosomes (independent assortment). In the fruit fly cross the two genes are on the same chromosome, so the model breeds far more flies with the parental combinations than the square predicts; in fruit flies only females cross over, so the heterozygote is the female. The table shows critical values for p = 0.10, 0.05 and 0.01; decisions use p = 0.05 (a 5 % probability that a difference this big is due to chance). A χ² test needs the numbers of offspring, not percentages, and works best when every expected number is at least 5.
