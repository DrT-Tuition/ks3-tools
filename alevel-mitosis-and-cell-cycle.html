Method and reasons

    Mitotic index = number of cells in mitosis (prophase + metaphase + anaphase + telophase) ÷ total number of cells. Multiply by 100 for a percentage.

    Time spent in a stage = (cells in that stage ÷ total number of cells) × length of the cell cycle. This assumes the cells are dividing at random times.

    Independent assortment: number of combinations = 2ⁿ, where n is the number of homologous pairs (the haploid number).

    Independent assortment: hidden for this question.

    About this model

    The model cell has 2n = 4 (two homologous pairs; chromosomes from one parent blue, from the other red) so every chromosome can be seen. Real cells have more: onion 2n = 16, humans 2n = 46; the plant Haplopappus gracilis really has 2n = 4. The DNA masses (8 pg per G1 cell) are invented. Phase lengths on the ring and the graphs are not to scale: in most cells interphase takes about 90 % of the cycle. The squash pictures are simplified drawings of a model root tip with 2n = 4 and invented counts: a real onion root tip has 2n = 16, so its dividing cells show many more chromosomes, and a real field shows many more cells. One crossover (chiasma) is drawn in each homologous pair. Stem cells (2.1.6 j) are undifferentiated cells that keep dividing by mitosis and can differentiate: a renewing source of new cells, such as the meristem in plants and bone marrow in mammals.
