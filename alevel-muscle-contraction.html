About this model

Lengths are typical values for mammalian skeletal muscle: myosin (thick) filaments 1.6 µm, actin (thin) filaments 1.0 µm from the Z line, a resting sarcomere about 2.5–3.0 µm. Real myofibrils have thousands of sarcomeres in a row and each myosin filament has about 300 heads that work out of step, so the pull is smooth; the model shows one head. The actin filament is really a twisted double chain with two tropomyosin strands, and each troponin binds more than one Ca²⁺. In the model ADP and Pi leave the head during the power stroke, as in the OCR course; in real muscle Pi leaves just before the stroke and ADP at the end of it. The neuromuscular junction is simplified: there are many more vesicles, channels and receptors, and the folds of the motor end plate are deeper. The sprint graph is a typical pattern, not one person’s data. The stained section is drawn, not a photograph.
