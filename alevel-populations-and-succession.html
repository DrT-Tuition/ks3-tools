Limiting factors

Abiotic: temperature, light, pH, water, O₂ and minerals.

Biotic: food, predation, disease, and competition (intraspecific and interspecific).

Predators and prey: see Predator–prey cycles.

About this model

Mark–release–recapture: each vole in the trapping area has a 2 in 5 chance of being caught in each sample (3 in 5 near the traps with "no mixing"); the marked voles are released into random places (or, with "no mixing", near the traps). The estimate N = (n₁ × n₂) ÷ m₂ assumes a closed population (no births, deaths, immigration or emigration between samples), marks that are not lost and do not change survival or behaviour, random mixing, and an equal chance of catching every animal; a real survey repeats the recapture and uses several traps placed at random. Succession: the stages are simplified; soil depth, number of species and biomass are shown as relative trends only, and real successions take hundreds of years and vary from place to place. Population growth: a model after Gause's Paramecium experiments (logistic growth to the carrying capacity; Lotka–Volterra competition), not real data. Handle live animals with care and release them where they were caught.
