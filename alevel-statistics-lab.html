What does the question ask about?
Are the two sets of measurements paired?

Not tests: standard deviation describes the spread of values (part 2); Simpson’s index measures diversity (part 5).

About this model

The data sets are realistic made-up samples; New data draws a new random sample each time, so the result is sometimes significant and sometimes not. Every decision uses p = 0.05 (a 5 % probability that a difference or correlation this big is due to chance) and two-tailed critical values. The t-tests assume the values are roughly normally distributed and the two groups have similar spreads; Spearman’s rank needs no such assumption, only values that can be ranked. In this model no two values in a column are equal; in real data, tied values share the mean of the ranks they would have taken. Printed tables of Spearman’s critical values differ slightly in the third decimal place; this one is the widely used table, checked against exact probabilities. Simpson’s index here uses D = 1 − Σ(n/N)², the OCR A version. χ² tests are in Inheritance and the χ² Test.
