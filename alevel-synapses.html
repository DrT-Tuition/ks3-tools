About this model

The synapse is drawn much bigger than life and simplified: a real knob holds hundreds of vesicles, and the cleft is about 20 nm wide. The model shows 8 steps one after another; in a real synapse release to binding takes under 1 ms, but making ACh again and refilling vesicles takes much longer. Ca²⁺ is pumped back out of the knob (using ATP) after each impulse; the model doesn’t show this. In Summation each EPSP is +10 mV and each IPSP −10 mV, the threshold is −55 mV and PSPs add exactly; a real neurone receives thousands of synapses, and where they are on it matters too. In Drugs the synapse is a strong one, where each impulse reaches threshold on its own. Drug effects are simplified: nicotine at high doses, and organophosphates, can also stop transmission by keeping the membrane depolarised. Curare and botulinum toxin mainly act at neuromuscular junctions (see the Muscle Contraction tool for the neuromuscular junction). For how an action potential is made, see the Action Potential tool.
