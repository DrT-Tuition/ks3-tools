Rule card

Series: one loop. The current is the same everywhere. The p.d. is shared between the bulbs. If one bulb goes out, they all go out.

Parallel: more than one loop. The current splits between the branches and adds up again. Each branch has the cell's p.d. If one bulb goes out, the others stay lit.

Cells in series add their p.d.s: each cell is 1.5 V.

Meter key

A is an ammeter. It measures current in amps (A) and sits in the loop.

V is a voltmeter. It measures potential difference in volts (V) and sits across a part, on its own side branch.

Model limits: wires, cells and meters are ideal. All the bulbs are identical. Each cell gives 1.5 V and cells in series add. A bulb's brightness shows the p.d. across it. Readings are rounded to 2 decimal places. Real batteries and bulbs are less tidy.

Remember: in a series circuit the current is the same everywhere and the p.d. is shared. In a parallel circuit the branch currents add up and each branch has the cells' p.d. An ammeter goes in the loop. A voltmeter goes across a part.
