About this model

Real particles are far too small to see, and there are billions of them. Here each dot stands for lots of particles, and the box is flat (2D). The clock is model time. Real gas particles move far faster: here they are slowed right down so you can follow one. The differences between cold and hot, and between nitrogen and argon, are made bigger so you can see them (real argon particles move only about 1.2 times slower than nitrogen). The air and water here are perfectly still. In the beaker, water particles are not drawn. Real times are much longer: in an ice bath the real crystal takes over 30 minutes.

Real dye particles are far too small to see, and there are billions of them. In the zoomed-in view each dot stands for lots of particles, and the view is flat (2D). The clock is model time: on a real plate the colours take a few minutes to reach the middle, and longer in cold water. The difference between warm and cold is made bigger so you can see it. Here the water is perfectly still; on a real plate the sugar and the warm water make it flow a little too.
