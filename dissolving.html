A new solute or solvent starts a fresh beaker of 100 g of solvent. The balance was set to 0 g with the empty beaker on it.

Model limits: Real particles are far too small to see, and there are billions of them. Here each particle stands for lots, and the numbers are not to scale. The solvent is always 100 g. Only the lesson's data is used: salt stays at 36 g per 100 g of water at every temperature. In real life you heat on a hot plate, not on a balance, and dissolving takes longer. Real solutions can be coloured, like squash or copper sulfate solution, but you can always see through them.
