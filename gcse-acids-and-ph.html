About this model

Ions and molecules are drawn far bigger than they are, and a few stand for billions; the water the solutions are made in is mostly not drawn. In the zoomed-in views one extra ion is NOT ten times more: each pH unit is really ten times the H⁺ concentration. In Strong and weak, ten times the concentration is drawn as only twice the particles, and about 1 in 8 weak acid molecules is drawn ionised; the real share is much smaller (about 1 in 75 for 0.1 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid), and in a real weak acid molecules keep splitting and joining again. Sulfuric acid is treated as giving 2 H⁺ for every molecule, as at GCSE; it and carbonic acid are not shown at 1.0 mol/dm³. The pH values are worked out for 25 °C; real meters need calibrating and real solutions vary a little. In Neutralisation the acid and alkali are both 0.10 mol/dm³, so exactly 20.0 cm³ of alkali neutralises 20.0 cm³ of acid. The colour chart is drawn; real universal indicator charts differ a little.
