About this model

Every model leaves something out. Dot-and-cross diagrams show which atom each electron came from, but all electrons are the same, and they don’t sit still at fixed points: the diagrams don’t show the shape of a molecule or the size of the atoms. The lattice picture is flat and shows only 24 ions; a real ionic lattice is 3D and goes on in every direction. The ball-and-stick pictures (sodium chloride, diamond, fullerene) show the gaps between particles far bigger than real, and the sticks are not real objects. The metal is drawn in flat rows; the bigger atoms in the alloy are given the same charge as the metal ions to keep the electron count simple. Melting points are rounded; diamond and graphite turn to gas rather than melting at normal pressure. The nanoparticle cubes are drawn at different zooms, not to scale.
