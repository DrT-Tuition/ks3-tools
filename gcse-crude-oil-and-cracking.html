About this model

Fractional distillation: crude oil is a mixture of a very large number of compounds; the model shows only straight-chain alkanes, as zig-zag chains far bigger than real molecules. The carbon numbers, temperatures and uses of the fractions are typical values: they vary between refineries and books. The model sorts every molecule exactly; in a real column the vapour and the liquid mix on every tray, so the fractions overlap. The largest molecules (bitumen) do not evaporate and drain from the bottom. The properties chart shows the trends only: viscosity and flammability are not measured values. Alkanes: only the names of the first four alkanes are needed. Cracking: the ball models show which atoms go where, not how the bonds really break. In the lab, liquid paraffin is soaked into mineral wool, and pieces of broken pot (or aluminium oxide) are the catalyst; take the delivery tube out of the bromine water before you stop heating, so it is not sucked back. Bromine water: a liquid hydrocarbon is added a few drops at a time and a gas is bubbled through, then the tube is shaken; a real liquid alkane does not mix with bromine water. Supply and demand: the percentages are illustrative values, not data for a real refinery.
