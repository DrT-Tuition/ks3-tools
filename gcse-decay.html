Model limits: The method follows the AQA required practical, using phenolphthalein (the current AQA sheet uses cresol red, which changes from purple to yellow). The times are invented example values. Each run has random scatter (about ±6 % in its time), and the time you record depends on when you press Stop (a little early or late), so a single run can mislead. About 1 run in 8 is an invented anomaly (for example, lipase not stirred in properly) that takes 60–90 % longer; there is never more than one among the runs at one temperature. Here an anomaly is a time more than 25 % away from the middle value of three. Time is sped up, and slows down near the end point so you can judge it. The milk tube warms in 2-minute steps, and the water bath stays at its temperature. Another way to follow decay: leave milk in beakers at each temperature and measure its pH each day with universal indicator or a pH meter. The colours are drawn to show the change, not exact shades. On the Decay factors tab the temperatures are simplified and the rates are invented, relative values.
