Model limits: The method follows the AQA required practical. Granite, lead and water have the densities in the lesson notes. The wood’s density (chosen inside the notes’ range for wood), liquid X, the sizes of the blocks and stones, the empty cylinder’s mass and the balance’s reading before you zero it are invented example values. Each ruler reading has a small random scatter (up to 0.3 mm, as real blocks aren’t perfectly square). About 1 length or width reading in 10 is an invented anomaly: the block isn’t lined up with the 0 mark. Each irregular-solid run has a small scatter (up to 0.2 cm³); about 1 run in 7 is an invented anomaly, where 2–3 cm³ of water clings in the spout or splashes out. About 1 liquid row in 7 is an invented anomaly, where 6–9 g of spilt liquid sits on the balance pan (wiped off before the next row). There is never more than one anomaly in a set of repeats. Here an anomaly is a value more than 3 mm, 1 cm³ or 3.5 % from the middle value. Read the cylinders to the nearest mark or half-way between two. The model ignores temperature, air bubbles and water soaking into a stone.
