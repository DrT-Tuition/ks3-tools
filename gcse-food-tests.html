Method and safety (from the lesson)

    Model limits: The lunch-box samples A to F (and Mystery sample X in Demo) are invented: what each contains is made up for practice, and they all look the same, as real food extracts can. Colours are idealised: real results can be pale or in between. Time runs fast: 1 minute takes 1 second. How fast the water bath heats and cools, how much a pipette squeeze delivers (0.3 to 0.7 cm³) and a drop (0.1 cm³) are model values. Benedict’s only changes colour in this model when the bath is above about 70 °C, and fully at 80–90 °C. The 2 cm³ measure is repeated for each of tubes 1–3. The lesson (s22) does the lipid test on the filtrate, but lipids do not dissolve in water, so fat stays behind in the filter: this tool tests a little of the ground food itself with ethanol, as in the standard method. There is no mean or graph: these tests give colours, not numbers.
