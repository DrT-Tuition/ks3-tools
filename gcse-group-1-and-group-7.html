About this model

In water: the observations are typical of a small piece of each metal in a trough of cold water with universal indicator, as in a teacher demonstration behind a safety screen. The colour spreading out from the metal shows the indicator changing as the product dissolves; the speeds are not to scale. Sodium can catch fire with an orange-yellow flame if it is held still, for example on filter paper. Rubidium and caesium are never put in water in school: the model gives predictions only. Air and chlorine: the tarnishing is sped up, and the particle picture shows only a few particles, far bigger than real. Lithium, sodium and potassium are stored under oil. Group 7: the colours are typical of dilute solutions; a few drops are added, so a halogen that does not react is diluted and looks paler. Chlorine water is used in a fume cupboard. Bromine and iodine have more electrons in their inner shells than the diagrams can show clearly, so only the outer shell is drawn with its electrons. The lines in the electron diagrams show how strongly the nucleus attracts the outer (or a new) electron: the thicker, the stronger. Boiling points are data book values, rounded to the nearest degree (helium, −268.9 °C, is shown as −270 °C).
