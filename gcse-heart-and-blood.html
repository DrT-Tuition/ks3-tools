About this model

Circulation: a plan, not a map; real blood takes about a minute to go all the way round, and the body has many organs side by side. Heart: a simplified cut-away front view, not to scale; blue shows blood with less oxygen (real blood is always red, dark red when it has less oxygen); the valves are drawn as two flaps each, and their names are not needed for GCSE. Pacemaker: the electrical wave is drawn as a glow; a real artificial pacemaker may have one, two or three wires. Blood vessels: the capillary is drawn far bigger than it really is; real veins are often squashed flat in a cross-section. Flow rate: a model with a pump and a tube; real blood flow is measured in other ways, but rate = volume ÷ time is the same. Blood: a stained smear; there are about 600 red blood cells for every white blood cell, so most fields of view show no white cells at all. Heart disease: the outside view of the heart is a textbook picture; the narrowing and treatments are simplified, and how fast fatty material builds up varies from person to person. A diet high in saturated fat, smoking and too little exercise all make cardiovascular disease more likely.
