About this model

Not to scale: white blood cells, bacteria, antibodies and toxins are drawn far bigger than they are, and a virus is much smaller than a bacterium. The shapes on the pathogens and on the antibody tips are a model: real antibodies are far too small to see with a light microscope. The antibody graph, the town and the medicine timelines are teaching models with made-up but sensible numbers: real responses vary from person to person and from disease to disease. In the town, a person can pass the disease to the eight people round them; real spread depends on how people mix. Monoclonal antibodies have their own tool (Monoclonal antibody maker).
