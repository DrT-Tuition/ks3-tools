Model limits: The readings (in units) are invented example values, not real data. They follow the order the method expects; real cubes can give closer readings for some faces. The detector shows all the infrared reaching it, including about 10 units from the room and the bench, so it never reads zero. Each reading has random scatter of about ±1 unit, so a single set can put shiny black and matt white the wrong way round. About 1 reading in 12 is an invented anomaly (a hand too near the detector) that reads 9–13 units too high, so about 3 sets in 10 have one; never more than one among the repeats of one face. Here an anomaly is a reading more than 4 units from the middle value of three. The reading depends on the distance (an invented rule: closer gives a higher reading); very close, the needle goes off the end of the scale. The water is treated as staying at the same temperature while you read the four faces; really it cools a little. Cooling flasks: invented cooling rates. Conduction and convection are the same for every flask; only the infrared emitted depends on the colour. Starting temperatures differ by up to 2 °C, each flask has a little random scatter, and there are no invented anomalies. Time is sped up (1 minute ≈ 0.7 s). The Leslie cube method follows the AQA required practical.
