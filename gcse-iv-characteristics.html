Model limits: The readings come from a model, not real results. The resistor and the lamp are modelled on the lesson’s sample results. The diode is an invented model: when its current starts to flow is a model value, and it never breaks down. Also invented: a supply of 0–6 V in 0.5 V steps, 0.2 Ω for the supply, leads and ammeter, a 0–20 Ω variable resistor, and a 5 Ω protective resistor with the diode. Each switch-on is a fresh contact at X, so readings scatter a little (under 1 %). For the resistor and the lamp, each switch-on at 2 V or more has a 1 in 10 chance of an invented poor contact (an anomaly: the pd reading is 35–55 % too high), at most 2 per component; the diode has none. The resistor warms if it is left switched on for a long time: its resistance rises 0.3 % for each °C (up to about 5 %), and it cools when switched off. The lamp’s filament heats up at once. The meters are shown as magnified straight scales, set to a suitable range for each component.
