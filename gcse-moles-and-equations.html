About this model

Particles are drawn far bigger than real, and only a few are shown. Ionic compounds (such as CaCO₃, MgO and NaCl) are drawn as small clusters of touching atoms, one formula unit each; really they are ions in a giant lattice, and the charges are not shown. On the balance, the 2.00 g of marble chips (calcium carbonate) react completely with excess acid; in a real flask a little carbon dioxide stays dissolved, so the reading falls a little less. The magnesium (0.48 g) all reacts; in a real crucible a little magnesium oxide smoke can escape when the lid is lifted, so a real gain can be smaller. In Reacting masses each particle stands for 0.1 mol, and the amounts change in steps of the big numbers in the equation. One mole of any gas fills 24 dm³ only at room temperature and pressure (20 °C and 1 atmosphere). Relative atomic masses are the ones on the AQA periodic table (Cl = 35.5, Cu = 63.5).
