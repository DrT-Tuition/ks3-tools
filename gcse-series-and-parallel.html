About this model

The cells and meters are ideal: a cell has no internal resistance, an ammeter has no resistance and a voltmeter takes no current. Real meters change a circuit a little. The lamp is given a fixed resistance of 6 Ω; a real filament lamp’s resistance rises as it gets hotter (see the I–V characteristics practical). The LDR’s resistance is typical: tens of kilohms in the dark and a few hundred ohms in bright light, so its current is shown in milliamps (1 mA = 0.001 A). The thermistor values are typical of a small one, rounded. Meter readings are rounded to 2 decimal places (3 below 0.1 A; 3 significant figures in mA), so shown values sometimes add up only approximately (≈).
