Model limits: The heater power (38–42 W, a little different each run), the heat losses, the thermometer’s response and the room temperature are invented example values with random scatter, not real results; about 1 run in 5 is an invented anomaly that loses extra energy. Time is sped up (1 minute = 1.5 s) and the clock pauses while you read the thermometer. The joulemeter readings every 2 minutes are recorded for you; you read it yourself at switch-off. The heater itself also warms up a little, so even with no heat loss c would come out 1–3 % high. A lighter block gets hotter, so it loses a bigger share of its energy and its measured c is further from the true value; the true c does not depend on the mass. Values of c are the lesson’s: water 4200, aluminium 900, sand 830, iron and steel 450, copper 385 J/kg °C.
