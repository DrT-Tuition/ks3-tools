About this model

Every model leaves something out. The road and the distances are to scale, but the car and the cow are not. The car slows with a steady deceleration: on a dry road with good brakes and tyres it is 6.25 m/s² (close to the Highway Code’s stopping distances); a wet road gives 3.125 m/s² (twice the dry braking distance), an icy road 0.625 m/s² (10 times), and worn brakes or tyres give 0.8 of these. Real cars, roads and drivers vary. The reaction times for a tired, distracted or drunk driver are examples: they vary a lot from person to person. Your own reaction time on a mouse, key or screen is quicker than moving your foot to a brake pedal. In the energy part all the kinetic energy goes to the brakes; really a little goes to the tyres and the air. When the car skids, the energy heats the tyres and the road as well as the brakes. In the momentum part a bounce loses no kinetic energy (a perfect bounce); real collisions lose some, but momentum is always conserved. Velocities are rounded to 2 decimal places.
