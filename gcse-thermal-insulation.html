Why the curve levels off: at first the water is much hotter than the room, so the temperature difference is big and energy is transferred quickly. As the water cools, the difference gets smaller, so it cools more slowly.

Mean temperature drop in 15 minutes

A smaller drop means the water cooled more slowly: a better insulator. This is a bar chart because the material is categorical (named types, not numbers); a line graph is for continuous data, such as temperature against time.

Model limits: The rates of cooling, the room temperature (19–21 °C) and the starting temperature (79–81 °C) are invented example values, chosen so that the mean drops in 15 minutes (1 layer, lid on) match the lesson’s example results. The model lumps every way energy escapes (through the sides, through the top, evaporation) into one cooling rate, and ignores the energy that warms the insulator itself. Each run has random scatter of about ±1 °C in its drop, so a single run can put two close materials the wrong way round. About 1 run in 7 is an invented anomaly (for example, a lid not pushed down properly), which drops 6–9 °C more; never more than one among the runs of one set-up. Here an anomaly is a drop more than 3 °C from the middle value of three. The effects of extra layers and of the lid are invented values. Time is sped up (1 minute ≈ 0.7 s) and the stopclock pauses at each reading. Insulation slows the energy transfer to the surroundings; it never stops it, and nothing makes energy. On the Walls tab the rates are relative, invented values.
