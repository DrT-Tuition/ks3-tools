      Model limits: Samples A–D, and their pH and dissolved solids, are invented example data. Time is sped up. Each mass of solid varies a little from run to run (about 2 %), and about 1 run in 8 gives an anomaly about a tenth too heavy; for samples with almost no solid it is too small for the balance to show. Some mistakes change your results too. The cylinder's marks, the heating speeds and the boiling tube's volume are invented. The method follows the AQA required practical, but with 25 cm³ of sample and about 2 cm of distillate (AQA uses 10 cm³ and about 1 cm); the pH meter and the condenser set-up are extras. A run more than 5 % from the middle value of the others counts as an anomaly, and normal scatter can occasionally put a good run there too. In the model, the salty water in the flask boils at 101 °C (real salty water boils a little hotter than water with nothing dissolved in it).
