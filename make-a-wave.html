Follow the red coil

The red dotted line marks the red coil's own place. The red dots show where it has just been. It moves back and forth around its own place. It never travels along the slinky.

Key words

  • Transverse wave: the coils vibrate at right angles to the direction the wave travels.
  • Longitudinal wave: the coils vibrate back and forth in the same direction the wave travels.
  • Peak and trough: the top and the bottom of a transverse wave.
  • Compression: where the coils are squashed close together. Rarefaction: where the coils are spread out.
  • Amplitude: the distance from the rest line to a peak, in metres (m).
  • Wavelength: the distance from one peak to the next peak (one whole wave), in metres (m).
  • Frequency: the number of waves passing a point each second, in hertz (Hz).

Feedback

Model limits: a real slinky has many more coils, and real sound waves move air particles you can't see. In this model the wave always travels along the slinky at the same speed. The fixed end soaks up the wave; a real fixed end would send it back. Push-and-pull movements are drawn smaller than up-and-down ones so the coils never pass each other.

Remember: a wave is a vibration that transfers energy from place to place without transferring matter. The red coil shows this: it only moves around its own place.
