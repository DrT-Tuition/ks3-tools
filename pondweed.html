    Going further (GCSE Higher): the inverse square law. This is beyond the KS3 lesson.

    Double the distance → the light intensity is a quarter (÷ 4).

    Light spreads out as it travels. At twice the distance, the same light covers 4 times the area. At three times the distance, it covers 9 times the area.

    Light intensity is proportional to 1 ÷ distance². Here it is compared with 10 cm = 1.

    About this model

    The bubble numbers come from a made-up model, not real pondweed: fewer bubbles as the lamp moves away. Real counts vary more from one minute to the next. Only the lamp lights the pondweed here. The stopwatch runs faster than real time, and the 2-minute wait is sped up even more. The light intensity follows the 1 ÷ distance² rule; real light intensity also depends on the lamp, and the rule fits best for a small bulb.
